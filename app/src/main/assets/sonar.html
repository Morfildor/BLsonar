How to read this scope

This runs a native Android BLE scan in low-latency mode and draws it here. It listens for advertisement packets — the little "I'm here" broadcasts from earbuds, watches, trackers, TVs, and phones nearby. Nothing is paired or connected to.

Bearing is not real. Bluetooth radios give signal strength, not angle. Each contact is parked at a fixed angle derived from its address so it stays put on the screen and is easy to track — but that angle tells you nothing about which way the device actually is.

What is real

  • Radius — distance estimated from RSSI via log-distance path loss, using advertised Tx power where available (default −59 dBm at 1 m, path loss exponent 2.2). Bodies, walls and pockets easily throw this off by 2–3×.
  • Colour — teal is far, amber is close, coral is very close.
  • The trail — the short arc behind each blip is the RSSI trend over the last few seconds. This is the honest part: walk a few steps and watch which contacts climb. Closing contacts are the ones you're walking toward.

Playing hot-and-cold

Pick a contact from the list to lock it. Walk ten paces, check the trend arrow, turn if it says ▽. Triangulating by walking beats any single reading.

Permissions

  • On Android 12 and later, only the Bluetooth scan permission is needed. The app declares neverForLocation, so Android does not ask for your location and none is read.
  • On Android 11 and earlier, the OS forces any BLE scan to hold the location permission. That is an Android rule, not a use of your position — nothing here reads GPS.
  • Scanning stops when the app goes to the background. Android throttles background scans hard, so a paused scope is better than a lying one.

Why contacts multiply

Phones and modern trackers rotate their Bluetooth addresses every ~15 minutes for privacy. The same handset will reappear as a fresh contact. Devices that hold a static address — cheap speakers, some sensors, older gear — stay put.

Demo mode

Demo fabricates plausible contacts with drifting signal strength so the scope can be tried anywhere. It's labelled SIMULATED on screen. Nothing shown in demo is from your surroundings.
